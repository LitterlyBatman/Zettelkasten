# zoroastrian religion

Zoroastrianism is one of the world's oldest known religions, founded around 3,500 years ago (roughly 1500 BCE) by the prophet **Zoroaster** (also called Zarathustra) in ancient Persia, which is modern-day Iran.

At its core, Zoroastrianism teaches a dualistic view of the world:

- There is a constant battle between **good** (represented by the god **Ahura Mazda**) and **evil** (represented by **Angra Mainyu** or Ahriman).
    
- Humans are seen as having free will, and they must choose between good thoughts, good words, and good deeds—or their opposites.
    

Key beliefs include:

- **Monotheism**: Ahura Mazda is the supreme, all-good god (though evil exists as a separate force).
    
- **Moral responsibility**: Each person has a duty to promote good and fight evil through their actions.
    
- **Afterlife**: After death, souls are judged; good souls cross a bridge to heaven, while bad souls fall into a hellish existence.
    
- **Fire and purity**: Fire is a symbol of truth and the divine. Zoroastrian temples often keep a sacred fire burning.
    

Zoroastrianism was once the dominant religion of the Persian empires (like the Achaemenid Empire under Cyrus the Great and Darius), but after the Islamic conquest of Persia in the 7th century CE, it declined.

Today, the largest communities of Zoroastrians are in **India** (where they are called **Parsis**) and in **Iran**. Their numbers are small—around 100,000 to 200,000 worldwide.
